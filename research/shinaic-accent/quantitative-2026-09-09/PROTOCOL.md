# Working protocol for the Shinaic accent analysis

This is a working record, not the final report. The report will be written after
the etymological review and quantitative analyses have been completed.

Work began 9 September 2026 at 05:07 UTC. The allotted eight-hour window ends
at 13:07 UTC. Scope: every Shinaic language represented in the existing Jambu
database, with dialect and source distinctions retained. Existing lexical data
are read-only; this project does not ingest or alter a source.

## Questions and units

1. Which independently described ancestral phonological and morphological
   properties predict modern accent location, mora accent, tone, and inflectional
   mobility? These are separate outcomes, not interchangeable labels.
2. Which predictions are deterministic in a stated domain, which are only
   tendencies, and which fail? Report all outcome categories and denominators.
3. What explains the apparent exceptions: distinct formation, contraction,
   quantity chronology, borrowing, source conflict, or an unresolved accent change?

The sampling unit is an etymological family, subdivided into formations where
necessary. Dialect forms and duplicate citations are evidence within a family,
not independent confirmations. Preserve the entire graph path and raw record.
Unlinked words remain in the coverage inventory; a meaning match alone is not
a cognacy assertion. Audit roots with no usable ancestral or modern accent too.

## Analysis sequence

- Freeze and hash the existing CLDF inputs and inspect all source conventions.
- Extract all seven living Shinaic languages and all linked ancestral families.
- Read each family and annotate its ancestral structure, formation, expected
  outcomes, attested results, conflicts, proposed explanation, and uncertainty.
- Cross-check the diagnostic and anomalous cases in the original descriptions.
- Tabulate classes defined from ancestral/grammatical features, independently
  of the modern outcome. Do not create a separate rule for each exception.
- Test broad proposals and restricted domains, report coverage and residuals,
  and use family-level validation to distinguish description from prediction.
- Freeze the analysis, then write the narrative report and complete appendices.
- Reproduce tables from the frozen inputs, reconcile counts, verify annotation
  coverage, and inspect the final report's presentation.

## Evidential controls

- An unmarked source form is not evidence of no accent or no tone.
- Short monosyllables do not distinguish early from late long-vowel accent;
  inflection can nevertheless distinguish fixed from mobile accent classes.
- The Sanskrit dictionary headword is not automatically the immediate ancestor.
  Alternate input formations require independent attestation or explicit status
  as a reconstruction; they cannot be selected simply to improve a rule's fit.
- Source disagreement is retained, with sensitivity calculations where needed.
- Modern aspiration, vowel quantity, and syllable count cannot automatically be
  projected to the stage at which an accentual change operated.
- A deterministic-looking small sample establishes a correspondence within that
  sample. It does not by itself establish an exceptionless historical sound law.
- Earlier notes are leads, not new verification. Each new check records what was
  actually read and the extent of the check.

## Deliverables

One final narrative report with class-by-language outcome tables; complete
annotated family dossiers; the complete record-level corpus and coverage audit;
source/notation register; explicit anomaly ledger; quantitative tables; code and
input hashes sufficient to reproduce every reported count.

## Scope clarification

The user explicitly authorized new etymological proposals during the investigation.
These remain research proposals, with segmental and semantic justification,
alternatives, confidence, and consequences for the accent analysis.
