P01 · gɑɬ; gaːɬ “skin”

Sv ← gā́tra (Turner 4124)

plausible lexical association; transmission unresolved

Analysis. Sauji gaːɬ is a strong candidate for the gā́tra family. Turner4124 independently includes Gawar-Bati gaλ skin and Dameli gadra, alongside the old limb/body meanings. Sauji lateral fricative reflexes in three and leaf provide a segmental comparison for tr, while the Gawar-Bati word supplies the precise semantic parallel. This identifies a plausible family but does not distinguish inheritance from regional borrowing; the virtually identical Gawar-Bati form makes contact a serious alternative.

Accentual consequence. No marked Sauji accent in these three records. Do not project old barytonesis onto an unmarked modern form or use retained aa to decide the contact history.

Discriminating evidence still needed. Compare dated Sauji and Gawar-Bati sources, local tr reflexes and the inflectional paradigm. Establish whether length and lateral quality match inherited Sauji correspondences.

Evidence. Turner4124, full cached original entry; Jambu Decker1992, HKAT and Knobloch2020 records. Gaw. is Gawar-Bati, not Gawri.

Status. Supplementary proposal, outside the frozen1686-family registry; no database edge added.

P02 · drab “grass”

Sv ← darbhá (Turner 6203)

plausible lexical association; inheritance versus contact unresolved

Analysis. Sauji drab can be compared with darbhá tuft of grass. The full Turner6203 entry provides Lahnda drabh, darabh, drabba and Ju. drab for creeping grass, as well as Sindhi ḍrabhu. These are independent segmental and semantic parallels for metathesis and loss of aspiration; the proposal is stronger than a grass-meaning match alone. A regional grass name could also have spread through contact, so a direct inherited Sauji reflex is not established.

Accentual consequence. The modern citation is unmarked. Its apparent consonant cluster does not license reconstruction of a modern accent or proof of OIA final-accent retention.

Discriminating evidence still needed. Identify the grass species and compare Sauji metathesis, aspiration and regional botanical loans. The general grass sense versus a particular tufted or creeping grass requires semantic verification.

Evidence. Turner6203 full cached entry, headword darbhá; Knobloch2020 Sauji record.

Status. Supplementary proposal outside the frozen family registry; no database edge added.

P03 · dyo'daar “cedar/deodar”

bro ← devadāru (Turner 6531)

strong regional lexical identification; likely contact or compound transmission

Analysis. The Brokskat cedar name has both the segmental structure and tree meaning of regional devadāru/deodar. Turner6531 supplies the compound rather than an unexplained simple root. Its final stress can belong to regional transmission of the compound and should not be used to reconstruct the accent of an otherwise inherited simple noun.

Accentual consequence. Final syllable stress is recorded; mora accent is unavailable in the Brokskat source. Whole-compound accent history remains unresolved.

Discriminating evidence still needed. Check local species naming and the immediate donor language, and compare the internally formed Shina compounds for deodar.

Evidence. Schmidt and Kaul2008 Table2 item158, primary reading; Turner6531 full entry.

Status. Supplementary proposal outside the frozen family registry; no database edge added.

P04 · joŋ / pɑ̃ “leg”

Ush ← jáṅghā / pāda (Turner 5082 / 8056)

plausible separate lexical identifications

Analysis. The merged Ushojo leg citation contains two different words. joŋ is a plausible jáṅghā leg/shank reflex, while pɑ̃ is a separate pāda foot/leg comparison. Segmental and semantic support must be assessed for each branch. This proposal does not extend the jáṅghā comparison to Shina gaan, which is not supplied by Turner5082.

Accentual consequence. Both forms are unmarked here. A slash-separated record cannot supply a single deterministic accent outcome.

Discriminating evidence still needed. Find primary Ushojo paradigms and establish the local shank/leg/foot semantic division and possible contact routes.

Evidence. Rensch, Decker and Hallberg1992 Ushojo wordlist; Turner5082 and8056 full entries.

Status. Supplementary split proposal; the raw merged record remains intact and no database edge is added.

P05 · 'ɽɪɳɖi “widow”

Ush ← raṇḍā/raṇḍī, branch5; compare *rēṇḍa (Turner 10593)

plausible regional lexical association; exact vowel history unresolved

Analysis. Ushojo initial-stressed ɽɪɳɖi belongs plausibly with the regional widow forms in Turner10593, especially branch5 raṇḍā/raṇḍī. The short high vowel and initial retroflex require a local correspondence or donor-language account; the *rēṇḍa alternative should not be selected from accent. Regional borrowing remains possible.

Accentual consequence. The initial syllable stress is real survey evidence, but the etymology does not provide a secure unique accented old input.

Discriminating evidence still needed. Compare regional widow terms, Ushojo r/ɽ behavior and the feminine inflectional paradigm.

Evidence. Rensch, Decker and Hallberg1992 Ushojo wordlist; Turner10593 full entry.

Status. Supplementary proposal outside the frozen family registry; no database edge added.
