# Working notes, not conclusions

## Source hierarchy and notation

Use Liljegren 2016 for the synchronic system and 2009 for the dialect chronology.
Use the current dictionary for lexical forms, but preserve discrepancies rather
than choosing the most convenient accent. Older transcriptions are not equivalent
measurements: Morgenstierne 1941 p. 11 explicitly did not recognize distinctive
tone. His lack of a contour mark cannot date tonogenesis.

Grammar PDF page number = printed page + 26. The extracted text in
`/tmp/palula-accent/liljegren2016.txt` records both. The PDFs and extraction are
temporary source copies, not lexical ingestions.

Only lexical stems have the final-three-vocalic-moras restriction (and verbal
stems the final-syllable restriction). Do not impose that window on entire
inflected words. Short monosyllables generally omit an acute by convention.
Source common transcription also uses i/u for some phonetic glides; do not treat
every written vowel as a separate syllabic nucleus. Final unaccented u represents
phonetic u or o (2016: 62–64), so it is not direct proof of an ancient u vowel.

## Well-supported layers to distinguish

1. Inherited accent opposition in unextended words, visible in vowel raising and
   inflection. Retention is not itself a sound change.
2. Apocope of accented final short vowels, with retraction to the last surviving
   vocalic mora: divasá > *deesá > deés, gōṣṭhá > *ghooṣṭá > ghoóṣṭ,
   putrá > putr. Long inflectional endings survive. Reconstruct the exact case
   morphology cautiously. Source: 2016 pp. 112–114.
3. Early open-syllable vowel changes: old accented aa > oo (Biori further uu),
   ee > ii, oo > uu; accented a > aa. Their relation is a chain shift, not a
   command to repeatedly feed all outputs through every rule. Later Ashret
   closed-syllable aa > oo and ee > ii affect FIRST-mora accent, a > aa affects
   short vowels. Biori has later closed-syllable oo > uu but retains a/aa/ee.
   Sources: 2009 pp. 20–24; 2016 pp. 78–80.
4. Late Ashret closed-syllable a-lengthening places accent on the second mora
   after aspiration (including h), OR when preceded by an unstressed syllable.
   Otherwise first mora: khaár, haát, basaánd vs báaṭ. The restriction to this
   layer matters: early open-syllable háata is falling. Need corpus audit.
5. Consonant loss, contraction, sonorant metathesis and de-syllabification change
   the vowel-bearing skeleton. Evaluate individually; do not invent a single
   unconditional 'all consonants disappear' rule.
6. Morphological extension and paradigm leveling can reset accent. Keep this
   distinct from phonetic sound laws, and avoid positing *-aka solely to rescue
   a counterexample.

Vowel quality can establish relative chronology: díiš 'village' requires accent
before ee > ii; phoó 'boy' has raising requiring earlier *phóo < *pháa before
the present late accent. Rising accent thus has multiple historical sources.

## Important counterexamples and ambiguities

- díiš < deśá has fixed stem accent despite the source oxytone. The grammar
  openly calls for an unexplained earlier accent shift. Check other formations.
- díir 'brother-in-law' is NOT a secure exception based on first source token
  devará: the full etymology also gives dḗvara. Audit alternatives before counts.
- tóoru 'star' is NOT a clean proof of retraction from tāraká: CDIAL 5798 also
  includes tā́rakā 'star' and tā́raḥ. Formation and semantics matter.
- bhruk 'kidney' < vṛkká is nonmobile, but CDIAL also compares vṛ́kya and other
  formations/possible contamination. Not a clean single-ancestor test.
- Dictionary bhróo 'brother' (Biori bhrúu) vs some 2009/2016 passages bhroó
  (bhruú). 2016 p. 66 itself gives bhróo. Source variation must be checked.
- ráat 'blood' has first-mora accent and mobile ratá; source rakta is unmarked,
  so do not silently add Vedic accent without verification.
- raát 'night as measure, 24 h' is linked to rā́trī but is second-mora and has
  reetí. Separate róot 'night opposed to day', fixed róota, has no source
  etymology; potential inherited rā́tri reflex and lexically split doublet.
  Establish historical attestations, morphology, and possible loans first.
- ac̣híi 'eye' < ákṣi: investigate inherited dual/paradigm sources; do not assume
  the dictionary stem alone predicts the accent of every historical case form.
- kháaṇ 'mountain' < khaṇḍá and yáandr 'mill' < yantrá are fixed-accent
  potential residuals. gh róoṇ (actual headword ghróoṇ) < ghrāṇá also.
- angóor < áṅgāra may support accent attraction by a later long vowel. Initial
  syllable is closed: distinguish vowel length from syllable weight.
- Teen numerals may involve their own morphological accent resetting. Do not
  equate compounded Sanskrit dictionary citation with direct unchanged ancestor.

## Recent research to evaluate independently

Samopriya Basu, SALA39 abstract 'Accentual archaisms and innovations in the
Indo-Aryan languages of the Hindukush–Karakoram', PDF pp. 31–33 in
`sala-2025.pdf`: inherited unextended barytone/oxytone opposition; accent
attraction from accented short vowel to later unaccented long vowel; extended
stems reset to final stem syllable (penult of extended form), e.g. kriṣíṇ-u/-i
< kṛṣṇá. Cite as conference abstract; not a full published demonstration.

Tegethoff 2025 thesis and Tegethoff & Basu in-press MSS article are mentioned on
an official FU Berlin page, but their contents have NOT been obtained/read.

Köhnlein's Shina paper is a synchronic foot-based analysis of Kohistani Shina,
not a diachronic reconstruction of Palula. It provides an alternative to
lexically specified tones; it does not prove that modern Palula has no inherited
accent. Its don/dódi 'tooth' vs don/doní 'flock' illustrates why short vowels
can preserve different underlying paradigmatic behavior.

## Corpus cautions

2,700 main dictionary entries; 511 cite Turner; 263 have acute in the FIRST
ancestral token. These are inventory counts, not independent historical samples.
187 noun/adjective/numeral entries in the first-token-accent subset. Some have
multiple source accents; some are derivatives of repeated ancestors. Need
manually coded evidence with morphological and source alternatives. The
reproducible script changes research outputs only, never the lexical database.

- **OCR correction:** Morgenstierne p. 13 §15 says long *u* (ū), not a general
  antepenultimate shortening of ā. The page has been visually checked. Do not
  use the false ā reading to explain bháaru.
- 2011 Vocabulary has headword **bhróo** but phonetic **[brhoó]** in the same
  entry. The later dictionary has first-mora accent in both fields. Brother is
  not a clean datum for dating an accent change without rechecking recordings.
- Eye **ac̣híi** has **first-mora íi** in its final syllable. MW 1899 p. 3 and
  Grassmann 1873 s.v. ákṣi attest Vedic dual **akṣī́**. This is a plausible
  immediate source with the right accent and final length; it requires dual
  lexicalization as a singular and gender remodeling. Schmidt's Shina data also
  have ac̣híi. No claim of priority for this proposal.
- CDIAL 10702 already includes Phalura **rōt**. Modern **róot** < rā́trī is a
  refinement of the current dictionary link, not a novel cognacy. Morgenstierne's
  noun was feminine, with plural rēti; modern róot is masculine. Modern feminine
  **raát**, plural **reetí**, has a distinct count/time meaning. The cause of
  the lexical/paradigmatic split remains unresolved.
- **bháaru** might continue short-vowel **bhára** (CDIAL 9393, with productive
  nominal extension) rather than **bhārá** (9459). Short a then undergoes early
  open-syllable lengthening. But *bháraka is unattested, and attested bhāraka has
  long ā; borrowing, remodeling and other shortening remain alternatives.
- **soór** source etymology loses a macron: CDIAL 13355 is **sā́ra**, also
  **sārá** per Pāṇini. Turner calls the 'ice' comparison phonetically doubtful
  and compares Wakhi. Do not count this as a secure sá > soó change.
- **yáab** source has yavyá, but CDIAL 10442 gives **yavyā́**. The first source
  token is not a flawless representation of the ancestral evidence.

## Dybo and inherited pre-Vedic differences

Read Dybo 2011, «Относительно др.-инд. -ya-глаголов», pp. 200–210, in
Journal of Language Relationship 6, published with Kulikov's paper and replies.
PDF pages = printed + 26, file dybo-vestnik2011.pdf:
https://www.rsuh.ru/upload/main/vestnik/fvir/Vestnik-16_11.pdf
This is not the separate pp. 92–167 Edelman Festschrift chapter.

Dybo pp. 202–206 gives fixed-accent Gilgiti nouns corresponding to Vedic oxytones,
including 'mill' (yantrá), 'kidney' (vṛkká), 'sleep' (nidrā́), 'pulse' (mudgá),
'cloud' (abhrá), and 'poison' (viṣá). Many have fixed-accent Palula counterparts.
He interprets some as preservation of older accent against Vedic innovation,
supported by other Indo-European branches. This is an alternative to repeated
Palula-specific retraction; his general tonal-morpheme theory is not established
by Palula alone.

Kulikov pp. 210–211 challenges the verbal reconstruction, citing weak/incorrect
etymologies and scarce decisive cognates. Do not extend that critique to every
noun comparison; do not present all noun comparisons as equally secure either.
Several Shina nouns visibly have derivational extensions.

Palula **biṣ / biṣá** 'poison' is mobile, unlike Dybo's fixed Gilgiti comparison.
Palula **dhut** means **mouth**, not 'milk': it is not Shina dut 'milk'.

## Late lengthening and verbal paradigms

Morphemic-short-a nouns with closed long-aa headwords mostly obey aspiration or
pretonic syllable → second mora, otherwise first. **dhráac̣** 'grape' has an
aspirated onset but first-mora accent; its OIA vowel was long, so the modern
short-a stem does not prove the historical input. **kháaṇ** 'mountain' is another
counterexample, without explicit morphemic short-a annotation. Selection only
by that annotation would hide it.

Explicitly listed simple-verb imperatives with long aa all have first-mora
accent in the inventory, including aspirated and polysyllabic forms: dháar,
háar, čháaḍ, biṣáam, utráap. The noun conditioning is not an unrestricted
synchronic rule for every closed syllable. Strong-verb stem-accent leveling is
plausible, but not an independently dated sound change. Count with audit script.
# Further etymological checks

Palula zaán 'self' (feminine), variant ǰaán, oblique ǰaní, is linked to
Turner jána- 5098 in the dictionary. Printed p163 of the 2011 vocabulary,
PDF209, checked in `/tmp/palula-accent/zaan-entry.png`. Palula separately has
ǰáanu 'person'. Possible alternative: a loan involving Persian/Urdu jān
'life, soul, self', or Pashto ځان dzān 'self'. The long vowel, late accent
and lexical z favor considering a loan over a straightforward jána inheritance.
Platts 1884 s.v. jān explicitly includes 'self' and gives gender s.f.m., not
exclusively feminine: https://www.rekhta.org/urdudictionary?keyword=jaa.n .
Pashto matches meaning and affricate better; Persian/Urdu matches the recorded
ǰaán variant. Counterevidence: ǰaní shows native mobile inflection, and Palula
zhaáy 'place' < Pashto dzāy uses a different consonant transcription. A loan
can join a native class, but the donor and route are not established. Do not
silently replace the dictionary etymology or invent a contamination as fact.

The closed-aa and imperative audits are in audit_palula.py. The 34 explicit
aa imperatives all have early accent, including aspirated and noninitial
roots. This is independently described by Liljegren 2016 p226, á→áa,
áa→óo, ée→íi, so it is not a newly discovered general sound law. A closed
short-a noun condition must not be projected onto imperative formation.
