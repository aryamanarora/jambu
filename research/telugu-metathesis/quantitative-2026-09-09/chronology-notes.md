# Chronology working notes — not the final report

## DEDR 277, perish/destroy: primary textual evidence

Sastri, Korada Mahadeva. 1969. *Historical Grammar of Telugu (with special reference
to Old Telugu, c.200 B.C.–1000 A.D.)*. Anantapur: Sri Venkateswara University.
Reading copy: https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.169957 .
The PDF offset varies: printed72/111 are PDF97/136 (+25), while the inscription
and index pages cited below are +27. Check each printed page rather than extrapolating.
OCR systematically mistakes the author's underlined l for plain l; use the images.

- p.350, word index: vowel-first aḻisina, aḻiputa versus initial-ḻ ḻassi,
  ḻaccina and numerous inflected forms. Jambu DEDR notation ṛ̆ represents this
  consonant, normalized only in analytical notation to ẓ. The author explicitly
  treats them as the same root with metathesis. Image PDF377 checked.
- p.311, inscription 38, Bezwada/Bezawada: ḻassi at line14; aḻisina spans
  lines20–21 after liṅgamb=a; aḻiputa at line35. Image PDF338 checked. These
  are different inflectional/derivational formations, so coexistence does not
  demonstrate free variation in an identical morphological context.
- p.310 headings assign inscription38 to **890 CE**; date attribution must be
  distinguished from a date read directly on the stone. Source bibliography:
  *Epigraphia Indica* XV:150–159,366–367. Printed heading image PDF337 checked.
- pp.291–292, inscription16, Nalajanampāḍu: heading assigns **670–680 CE**;
  ḻacchina-/ḻaccina- at lines18–21. Earlier proof of displacement preserving ḻ.
  Source *Epigraphia Indica* XXVII:203–206, Alfred Master's edition. Date is
  a scholarly attribution, not treated as a precise year of the sound change.
  Heading PDF318 and actual text PDF319 both visually checked. Sastri's text
  at line18 and line21 prints underlined l in laccina; DHARMA's first token has
  the alternative doubled-ch transcription ḻacchina.
- Independent digital edition: Jens Christian Thomas, DHARMA, inscription
  `DHARMA_INSTelugu00026`, Nalajanampāḍu grant of Bādirāju, lines18–21.
  https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSTelugu00026 . XML snapshot commit
  `9cd04e21aa21e9f401090a7db76d25675cce5a27`, downloaded2026-09-09.
  Apparatus contrasts Butterworth/Chetty's ḍaccina with the current ḻ reading;
  the distinction is an editorial reading problem, not automatically two ancient
  dialectal variants. Preserve the competing editions.
- DHARMA00099 (grant at Inpuḻōli), line3 ḻaccinavānṟu, also confirms that
  initial ḻ readings are not confined to a single damaged monument. Its XML
  publication/revision date is modern and must never be used as the inscription date.

Inference: metathesis-family displacement predates the later ẓ/ḻ→ḍ outcome in
this lexical family. K03 p.195 reconstructs high-i plus transitive *ntt/mpp
formations; if cc arose by front-vowel-conditioned palatalization, that change
precedes the deletion of i. Neither inference establishes one family-wide date
for all metathesis, nor continuous productivity until the last surviving variant.

## Productivity and distinct later processes

- Sastri p.72 (PDF97, visually checked) says native metathesis had ceased as an
  active formative principle in Middle Telugu, while literary doublets persisted.
  His Middle Telugu is 1000–1600, not a label for present-day Telugu. Examples
  include elā-dīgalu/lē-dīga, eraka/rekka and aḍaṅgu/dāgu. These are source claims
  to compare with actual dated attestation and morphology, not proof of uniform
  cessation at a calendar boundary.
- The same page lists anu 'say' formations anan/nān, anābaḍu/nābaḍu,
  anavuḍu/nāvuḍu, anaka/nāka, anaru/naru. Nasal participation in a specific
  grammatical family must be kept distinct from the main nonnasal apical rule.
  Source also explicitly notes metanalysis adding n in negayu/egayu and
  nuṇḍi/uṇḍi, so an initial nasal is not by itself proof of metathesis.
- Sastri p.72 lists Druggādēvi (SII V1217, Ganj,1290) as sporadic metathesis,
  alongside separate h-cluster reversals Brahma/Bramha and vahni/vanhi.
  DHARMA00101 independently discusses druggādēvi and mora preservation, but is a DIFFERENT Lakshmipuram inscription, source-dated c.681 by Sastri p.294; it must not inherit the 1290 date of SII V1217.
- Sastri p.74 (PDF99) describes initial r-cluster loss in coastal speech versus
  retention in conservative border areas, and long-vowel-plus-nasal loss in
  standard speech versus nasal retention in the southern Madras region. Those
  source-relative dialect descriptions must not be presented as a new 2026 survey.
- Sastri p.111 (PDF136) directly gives dappi thirst < dayyu weary and compares
  noccu/noppi, puccu/puppi nominal formations. This is a morphological alternative
  for d109 to be tested against d447 and southern thirst words. It is separate
  from homophonous destruction ḍappi (d277).
