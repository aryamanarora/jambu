# Kui–Kuvi vowel lowering: examples, controls, and dependencies

This audit follows the **18 numbered positive examples** in Krishnamurti
1980, pp. 496–498, their distribution table on p. 499, and the controls and
residuals on pp. 500–504. It compares the arguments with Subrahmanyam 1983,
pp. 220–224, and the original DEDR articles. It is a review of a published
argument, not a new lexical-source installation or an exhaustive modern
Kui–Kuvi corpus.

The correspondence under examination is **earlier `e…a/o…a → ā` in affected
contractions**. Krishnamurti proposes open-mid contraction vowels followed
by lowering; Subrahmanyam proposes lowering before contraction. A printed
long vowel is an observation; the suffix vowel, reconstructed intermediate,
etymological grouping, and chronology may each be separate hypotheses.

## The 18 positive examples

Forms below are selected discriminating observations, not complete copies of
the source's etymological groups. `∅` means no form supplied in the cited
comparison, not proof that the language lacks the word. DED numbers used in
1980 differ from modern DEDR numbers; the links below use DEDR.

| K80 item and modern group | Relevant comparison | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Scoop up, `d1959`** | Kui `grāpa` beside dialectal `grēpa`; Konda `ker-`, Kuvi `grec-`. | Good evidence for a regional `ā/ē` outcome contrast. The proposed exact `*ker-a` formative is still an input claim. The Kui variants are useful evidence that the distribution was not uniform. |
| **2. Young, `d513`; competing `d291`** | Kui `lāvenju`, `lāa`; Kuvi `rā'a/lāa`; Telugu `ela/lē-`. | The initial lateral and contraction comparison are useful, but K80 n. 3 explicitly moves the Kui–Kuvi words from earlier `*aḷ-a` ‘strength’ to `*eḷ-a` ‘young’ partly on the strength of the lowering rule. DEDR 513 explicitly cites K80 for that reassignment. It cannot be an independent second confirmation of the same rule. |
| **3. Silk-cotton tree, `d495`** | Kuvi `ḍākāngi` beside `lēko`; Konda `lēka`, Kannada `elava`. | K80 explicitly reports no Kui cognate for this item. The distinct derivatives and Kuvi dialects should be retained; the `ā/ē` difference is not a single matched word under identical conditions. |
| **4. Salt, `d2674`** | Gondi `sowar/hovar`, Konda `sōru`, Pengo `hōr`, Kui `sāru`, Kuvi `hāru`, Manda `jār`. | A strong quality comparison involving loss of intervening `w`, rather than apical displacement. Initial `s/h/j` is a separate history. The comparison establishes the correspondence more firmly than the relative order of lowering and contraction. |
| **5. Descend, `d516` versus `d2456`** | K80 groups Kui `jāpa` with Konda `ṟe-`, Kuvi `rec-/re'-`, causative `rēph-`, and Manda `jē-`. | Two problems: p. 499 counts a shared Kui–Kuvi lowering, but its printed Kuvi forms have no `ā`. More substantively, DEDR places Kui `jāpa` and Manda `jē-/hē-` with Tamil `cāy` ‘incline’ in 2456, citing Burrow 1976 pp. 40–41, and keeps the other descent comparison in 516. The proposed apical origin of Kui `j` therefore competes with an inherited initial `c` history. Do not count it as a secure positive without resolving that comparison. |
| **6. Crow/lament, `d1574`** | Kui `klāpa`; Kannada `kele`, Tamil `cilai`; Kuvi `klīri` and related expressive formations. | Supports the proposed low-formative comparison, while Kuvi's different formation is not a straightforward failure to lower the same input. Its historical vowel and expressive consonant developments remain separate. |
| **7. Hollow/pit, `d1818`** | Kui `krāu`, Kuvi `gṛāyu/glāyu`, beside Kui `krōdu` ‘tube’ and Pengo `krōy`. | Strongly relevant quality contrast, but different meanings and derivatives matter. K80 itself notes the Kui ‘pit/tube’ distinction. The presence of both vowels does not identify one universal conditioning from the surface alone. |
| **8. Cut, `d1859`** | Kui `krāpa`; Tamil `kuṟai`, Kannada `kore`. | A useful apical-plus-low-formative comparison. The different stop/rhotic realizations and verbal formatives require their own histories. |
| **9. Family/kindred, `d2018`** | Kui `klāmbu`, plural `klāpka`; Kannada `keḷe`, Tamil `kiḷai/kēḷ`. | The low-formative proposal is plausible; the medial `mb/pk` alternation belongs to the paradigm. K80 p. 497 prints **1628**, but the relevant old DED number is **1678**, as confirmed in DEDR 2018. Old DED 1628 is the unrelated ‘do’ group, now DEDR 1957. |
| **10. Open, `d3259`** | Kui `dāpa`; Kuvi `de'-`, Konda `ṟe-/tere-`, Telugu `teṟacu`. | The quality contrast coexists with unequal displacement and later first-member loss. It illustrates why those operations need to be separated. |
| **11. Moon, `d3754`** | Kui `ḍānju`; Kuvi `lēnju`; Telugu `nela`; Konda `nela/lēŋ`. | Relevant `ā/ē` contrast, with lateral/retroflex development and loss of the original initial nasal in the changed forms. Telugu's unchanged `nela` is structurally outside its narrower cluster-producing rule. A retained long vowel before a nasal–stop also cautions against universal shortening across languages. |
| **12. Sell, `d4536`, separated from `d3949`** | Konda `por-`, Pengo `pro-`, Manda `pre-`; Kui `prāpa`, Kuvi `prā-`. | A good regional comparison once the ‘sell’ set is separated from ‘spread’. This is a positive source-history result: DEDR 4536 explicitly attributes the separation from old DED 3255 to Krishnamurti 1980. Its wider Proto-Dravidian ancestry is not established merely by the former grouping. |
| **13. Rice, `d3982`** | Kui `prāu/prāma`, Telugu `prālu`; Konda `per(u)/perku`, Gondi `paṛek/perek`, Tamil `parukkai/peṟukkal`. | The comparison supplies an apical and real `a/e` variation, but it does not independently force one `*per-a` input for every language. Telugu `ā` and the varying `k/l` formatives are a warning against treating every `ā` in this set as the specifically Kui–Kuvi innovation. |
| **14. Name, `d4410`** | Kui (Keller) `pāru`, Konda/Telugu `pēru`, Tamil `peyar`. | Contraction across a glide provides a second non-apical test. The source's restricted Kui dialect matters. Other ‘name’ formations, including the `dōr/tōr` series, should not be mechanically derived from the same complete stem. |
| **15. Hare, `d4968`** | Konda `morol`, Pengo `mrōl`; Kui `mṛāḍu`, Kuvi `mṛālū`. | Useful quality comparison. K80's `*moẓ-al` and PSS83's `*mol-al`, with lateral dissimilation in Konda/Pengo, differ in deeper consonant analysis. The modern initial cluster alone cannot settle that choice. |
| **16. Rope, `d4976`** | Kui `mrāsu`; K80 Kuvi `mārcca`, alongside Gondi `maronj/moros` formations. | The low outcome is clear in Kui. DEDR and PSS83 instead print Kuvi (Ṭ.) `marcu` with a short vowel. This need not be the same dialectal token, but the quantity discrepancy prevents treating the sources as interchangeable confirmations. |
| **17. Plaster/smear, `d5082`** | Kui `mrānḍa`; Telugu `mrē̃gu/mrēvu`; Tamil `meẓuku`, Konda `miṛis-`, Kuvi `mrispi`. | Strong evidence for an apical comparison, weaker evidence for an identical low-vowel formative. K80's `*meẓ-a` and PSS83's explicit parenthesized `(a)` are reconstructions; the observed high-vowel derivatives do not independently establish that `a`. |
| **18. Last year, `d5153`** | Kui `ṛānḍu` beside `ṛōnḍu` ‘a previous year’; Kuvi `ṛāṇḍu` types; Konda `nṛund`. | Compound and deictic formation matter. On p. 498 the second language label is printed ‘Kui’ again; DEDR separately attests Kuvi forms. The later dictionary helps establish the actual language, but one should disclose rather than silently reproduce the ambiguous source label. |

## Controls and apparent counterexamples

| K80 item / additional control | Observation | What it decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **19. Skin, `d3559`** | Kui `ṭōḍu`, Kuvi `tōlu`, and southern `tōl` retain `ō`; compared with southern `tokal/togal` types. | If the proposed earlier contraction is right, its chronology must differ from the affected contractions. A source label ‘contracted’ alone does not predict `ā`. This is consonant loss, not necessarily apical displacement. |
| **20. Fire, `d2929`** | Kui `drē`, compared with `*ner-u` and southern `neruppu`. | A high-formative control against unrestricted lowering of all contracted mid vowels. It also has its own initial-cluster reduction. |
| **21. River/water, `d5159`** | Kui `ēju`, Kuvi `ēyu`; the source reconstructs a long mid vowel at the South-Central stage. | An already long mid vowel need not lower. Its deeper relation to `yāṟu` belongs to another comparison; this is not proof that every ancestral stage had `ē`. |
| **22. Burn, `d811`** | Kui `ērpa`, with long- and short-root formations elsewhere. | A control using an older long vowel, whose root allomorph must be kept distinct from newly contracted short-root forms. |
| **23. Stick, `d2237`** | Kui `kōḍ`, Kuvi `kōlu`, southern `kōl`. | A particularly clear inherited-long-vowel control: no low-vowel formative is needed to create the long vowel. |
| **24. Chironji, `d2628`** | Kui `srēko`, Kuvi/Manda `rēko`, Gondi `sarēka`, Central Dravidian `sire/cīre/cīr`. | K80 calls it an exception under `*cir-a`. The actual initial quantity and derivative are reconstruction questions; the long Central forms prevent treating that input as directly attested. It is an apparent exception conditional on the proposed input. |
| **25. Bone, `d4418`** | Kui/Kuvi `pṛēnu`; Konda `peṛen`, Gondi `peṛeka/peṛeŋka`. | K80's `*peẓan` would raise a lowering problem, but the recorded uncontracted Konda form has **e**, not **a**, in the second syllable. Earlier `a → e` assimilation is possible; original non-low quality is also a live alternative. The long output does not independently prove the low input whose failure it is meant to test. |
| **Fat, `d2146`**, PSS83 p. 236 | Kui `krōga`, Konda `koṛvu`, Pengo `krō-`; southern `koẓu/koẓuppu`. | Another high-formative control, usefully compared with ‘hollow’ above. The specific `*koẓ-uw-` analysis belongs to derivative reconstruction; this example is not one of K80's numbered 18 positives. |

## What can be counted

The **itemized table** on K80 p. 499 assigns nine items to Kui only, one
to Kuvi only, six to Kui and Kuvi, and two to Kui, Kuvi, and Manda. That is
18 listed items, with 17 assigned to Kui, nine to Kuvi, and two to Manda.
The adjoining prose instead says 18/18 for Kui and ten Kui-only items.
Its own “no Kui cognate” item makes that arithmetic untenable.

Even the mechanically corrected numbers are **not validated rates of a sound
law**. Item 5 conflicts with both its printed Kuvi forms and a competing
etymology; item 2's membership depends partly on the rule; Manda is assigned
to item 2 in the summary without a Manda token in the displayed item; and
quantity or language labels need checking in items 16 and 18. Dialectal
variants also mean ‘shows the change somewhere’ differs from ‘changes
uniformly’. The 18 positives exclude the controls and residuals by design.

The qualitative conclusion survives: several comparisons support a restricted
low-formative `ā` outcome in Kui–Kuvi, and a blanket `ē/ō → ā` rule fails.
The exact diffusion percentages and the intermediate phonetics do not follow
from this small, partly theory-selected list. This judgment is compatible
with retaining the useful etymological separations the paper proposed.
