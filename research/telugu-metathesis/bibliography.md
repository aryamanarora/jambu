# Sources and access record

For **What moved in Telugu metathesis?**, researched 8 September 2026.
Page references are printed pages unless explicitly described as PDF pages.
“Full text available” describes access, not a claim to have read every page
of a book. The sections relevant to the argument were read; difficult
transcriptions, rule tables, and disputed references were checked against
page images. Downloaded books are working references and are not distributed
with this supplement.

## Principal comparative analyses

1. **Krishnamurti, Bhadriraju. 1961. _Telugu Verbal Bases: A Comparative and
   Descriptive Study_.** University of California Publications in Linguistics 24.
   Berkeley and Los Angeles: University of California Press. **K61** in
   the casebook. [Google Books, later reprint with original pagination](https://books.google.com/books?id=56PXtopfoscC&pg=PA51).
   Read pp. 51–68 and the corresponding notes on pp. 129–131, plus p. 28,
   §1.69, and the contrasting etymological entries on pp. 437 and 445
   for *pēlu/prēlu*. These are the principal source for immediate derivative structure,
   quantity alternatives, initial *m → b*, probable early Kannada loans,
   and the proposal of secondary *r* in *truḷḷu*. Google Books page images
   were inspected directly; a complete downloadable PDF was not obtained.

2. **Krishnamurti, Bhadriraju. 2003. _The Dravidian Languages_.** Cambridge:
   Cambridge University Press. **K03**. [Full-text copy](https://tamilnavarasam.in/Books/Others/The_dravidian_languages.PDF).
   Especially pp. 99–117, 128–131, 150–163, 188–198, 222–223, 259–265,
   and 483–484. The detailed metathesis synthesis is pp. 157–163.
   Introductory lists on pp. 8–13 and the compound discussion on p. 202
   were checked for the reconstruction audit. In this copy, printed page
   *n* is PDF page *n* + 6. Extracted text corrupts several phonetic symbols;
   images were used for disputed readings.

3. **Subrahmanyam, P. S. 1983. _Dravidian Comparative Phonology_.**
   Annamalainagar: Annamalai University. **PSS83**.
   [Complete scanned book](https://archive.org/details/SubrahmanyamDravidianComparativePhonology1983).
   Chapter 16, pp. 225–248, is the principal countercheck on the scope,
   mechanism, unchanged forms, and non-South-Central comparisons. Also
   pp. 220–224 on Kui–Kuvi lowering and pp. 81–87, §6.3, on Kota glide
   metathesis. Printed page *n* is PDF page *n* + 30 in the downloaded scan.
   Chapter 16's etymological numbering must not be mistaken for DEDR IDs.

4. **Krishnamurti, Bhadriraju. 1980. “A Vowel-Lowering Rule in Kui-Kuvi.”**
   _Proceedings of the Sixth Annual Meeting of the Berkeley Linguistics
   Society_, 495–506. **K80**.
   [Publisher's full text](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/BLS/article/view/2099),
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3765/bls.v6i0.2099).
   Entire paper read and rule/example pages visually checked. The distinction
   between low-vowel and high-vowel contractions is central; the itemized
   distribution on p. 499 does not support the prose's count of Kui cases.
   The separate vowel-lowering audit checks all 18 positives and the
   controls against later DEDR grouping, including the disputed ‘descend’
   comparison, the theory-dependent ‘young’ reassignment, and the useful
   separation of ‘sell’ from ‘spread’. Corrected label counts are not
   treated as validated diffusion rates.

5. **Krishnamurti, Bhadriraju. 1978. “Areal and Lexical Diffusion of Sound
   Change: Evidence from Dravidian.”** _Language_ 54(1): 1–20.
   [DOI and abstract](https://doi.org/10.2307/412996).
   **Abstract accessed; full article not obtained.** The six percentages
   reported in the essay were checked in the author's 2003 synthesis,
   p. 160, which cites the 1978 paper's p. 9. His Konda stress analysis
   is also quoted in the 2003 synthesis and discussed in PSS83. The paper
   was reprinted with an addendum in _Comparative Dravidian Linguistics_
   (2001), pp. 160–182:
   [reprint abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198241225.003.0010).
   That chapter was also access-restricted. No claim here rests on having
   independently re-counted the complete 1978 data matrix.

6. **Ramaswami Aiyar, L. V. 1932. “Aphaeresis and Sound-Displacement in
   Dravidian.”** _Quarterly Journal of the Mythic Society_ 22: 448–480.
   [Scanned volume](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.53140).
   Read the article's comparative lists and argument, especially
   pp. 449–455 and 468–479. This is the earlier assimilation/accent account,
   including different vowel-retention outcomes and unchanged controls.
   OCR is highly unreliable for forms. The cited concluding argument was
   checked in the scan; the article's bibliographic end page does not imply
   an independently read p. 480 in the working extraction.

7. **Emeneau, M. B. 1970. _Dravidian Comparative Phonology: A Sketch_.**
   Annamalainagar: Annamalai University.
   [Tamil Digital Library scan](https://tamildigitallibrary.in/assets/docs/uploads/primary_files/book/TVA_BOK_0012142_Dravidian_comparative_phonology.pdf).
   Read §15, pp. 33–36 (PDF pp. 42–45). Useful independent formulation
   of quantity, contraction, and the limitations of later cluster loss.
   References are to the older DED edition.

## Mechanism and other Dravidian languages

8. **Blevins, Juliette, and Andrew Garrett. 2004. “The Evolution of
   Metathesis.”** In Bruce Hayes, Robert Kirchner, and Donca Steriade (eds.),
   _Phonetically Based Phonology_, 117–156. Cambridge University Press.
   [Author-hosted chapter](https://linguistics.berkeley.edu/~garrett/Blevins-Garrett-2004.pdf).
   Full text consulted. Note 9 connects South-Central apical displacement
   with the perceptual account; discussion of other metatheses should not
   be mistaken for evidence of every Telugu intermediate.

9. **Hume, Elizabeth. 2002. “Predicting Metathesis: The Ambiguity/Attestation
   Model.”** September 2002 manuscript, Rutgers Optimality Archive 546.
   [Full preprint](https://roa.rutgers.edu/files/546-0902/546-0902-HUME-0-0.PDF),
   [repository record](https://roa.rutgers.edu/article/view/556.html).
   Read the Kuvi analysis, pp. 24–25, and surrounding mechanism discussion.
   The argument uses Israel's lexicon to compare word shapes, while allowing
   disfavored shapes to exist. The corresponding final article appeared
   in _Language_ 80(2): 203–237 (2004),
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1353/lan.2004.0083), but its complete text was
   **not obtained**. Page references in the essay are to the 2002 manuscript.

10. **Hume, Elizabeth. 2001. “Metathesis: Formal and Functional
    Considerations.”** Manuscript, Rutgers Optimality Archive 492.
    [Full preprint](https://roa.rutgers.edu/files/492-0202/492-0202-HUME-0-0.PDF).
    The Kui stop-cluster discussion provides background to the distinction
    between liquid displacement and consonant–consonant reordering. Its
    perception experiment is not direct phonetic evidence from early Telugu.

11. **Garrett, Andrew, and Juliette Blevins. 2009. “Analogical
    Morphophonology.”** In Kristin Hanson and Sharon Inkelas (eds.),
    _The Nature of the Word: Studies in Honor of Paul Kiparsky_, chapter 22.
    [Author-hosted chapter](https://julietteblevins.ws.gc.cuny.edu/files/2013/04/GarrettBlevins2009d-AnalogicalMorphophonology.pdf).
    Read especially §22.4 on Kondh *kp/pk*, *gb/bg*, and morphological
    analogy. This is a different problem from Telugu apical displacement.

12. **Winfield, W. W. 1928. _A Grammar of the Kui Language_.** Calcutta:
    Asiatic Society of Bengal.
    [Complete scan](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/37202.pdf).
    Read pp. 1–2 on adjacent vowels and p. 10 on plural formations,
    including *kiru : krīka* and *priu : prīnga*. Forms and length marks were checked
    against images. Kui hiatus establishes a parallel, not an ancestral
    Telugu attestation.

13. **Steever, Sanford B. (ed.). 2020 [ebook released 2019]. _The Dravidian
    Languages_. Second edition.** Routledge.
    [Book DOI](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315722580).
    Complete user-local PDF available; the title verso says 2020.
    Consulted the [Telugu chapter](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315722580-10)
    by Bhadriraju Krishnamurti, pp. 260–295, especially loan vocabulary
    p. 261; Konda pp. 296–297; Kuvi pp. 352–355; and Peri Bhaskararao's
    Gadaba chapter, pp. 408–410, for the Koṇekor Gadaba *savl-/saluv*
    paradigm and associated vowel insertion. This last example is **not
    Parji**.
    The essay cites printed pages, independently of the download's filename.

14. **Emeneau, M. B. 1997. “Brahui Etymologies and Phonetic Developments:
    New Items.”** _Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies_
    60(3): 440–447. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X00032481).
    Full user-local article consulted, especially p. 445, §12, on ‘sift’.
    The 1971 study it cites was not obtained in full; its older seven-item
    series was checked through PSS83, pp. 244–245. The later article adds
    evidence and prevents treating seven as a current exhaustive count.

15. **Kujur, Anup Kumar, and Biswanandan Dash. 2025. “Peculiarities of
    Metathesis in Kunha Language.”** _Jurnal Arbitrer_ 12(2): 114–124.
    [Full article](https://arbitrer.fib.unand.ac.id/index.php/arbitrer/article/view/735),
    [DOI](https://doi.org/10.25077/ar.12.2.114-124.2025).
    Entire paper read. Selected Kurux–Kunha pairs are useful observations;
    inconsistent segment descriptions and unaccounted vowel differences
    prevent adopting its broadest formulation as a uniform historical law.

## Borrowing, dictionaries, and reconstruction provenance

16. **Burrow, T., and M. B. Emeneau. 1984. _A Dravidian Etymological
    Dictionary_. Second edition.** Oxford: Clarendon Press. **DEDR**.
    [Digital dictionary](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/burrow/),
    [introduction](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/burrow/frontmatter/frontmatter.html).
    Read §§2–9 of the introduction and the entries identified throughout
    the casebook. The repository's preserved original article text was
    consulted alongside extracted CLDF rows for qualifications and source
    grouping. DED numbers in earlier publications require conversion.

17. **Brown, Charles Philip. 1903. _A Telugu-English Dictionary_.**
    Second edition, revised by M. Venkata Ratnam and W. H. Campbell.
    [Digital page 569](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/brown_query.py?page=569).
    The entry records *trāsu/tarāsu/tarāju*. Brown's language label alone
    is not the evidence for the proposed foreign origin; compare the
    Telugu chapter in Steever's volume.
    Also checked *uramu* ‘chest’, p. 168, labeled Sanskrit, and
    [*rommu*, p. 1096](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/brown_query.py?qs=%E0%B0%B0%E0%B1%8A%E0%B0%AE%E0%B1%8D%E0%B0%AE%E0%B1%81&searchhws=yes&matchtype=exact).
    The latter's etymological parenthesis actually prints *urumu* in Telugu
    script. The source spelling and unresolved quantity are disclosed in
    the casebook; the two entries are not silently made identical.

18. **Southworth, Franklin C. “Prehistoric Implications of the Dravidian
    Element in the NIA Lexicon, with Special Attention to Marathi.”**
    Author-hosted manuscript.
    [Full text](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/~fsouth/DravidianElement.pdf).
    Read the contact argument and visually checked table 1, PDF p. 9:
    *ḍoke/ḍoi*, *ḍāv-*, and explicitly **PSD2** *lenk-*.
    The running filename is not the paper's title. The manuscript does
    not itself establish a publication year; it is cited without assigning
    one from unrelated metadata.

19. **Southworth, Franklin C. “Proto-Dravidian Agriculture.”**
    Author-hosted manuscript.
    [Full text](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/~fsouth/Proto-DravidianAgriculture.pdf).
    The reconstruction list on PDF p. 24 assigns ‘dog’ to DEDR **2916**.
    The local legacy row points to **916**. The methods discussion is also
    relevant to deciding a reconstruction's historical depth.

20. **Merriam and Fuls. 2026. Dravidian database release documentation.**
    [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/21433491),
    [README](https://zenodo.org/records/21433491/files/README.md?download=1).
    Checked the documented composition of the dataset and compared the
    installed source rows and import paths in Jambu. Attribution to a
    republished author is not an independent vote for a reconstruction.
    The inventory's counts describe the installed snapshot, not every row
    in the upstream release.

21. **Starling Dravidian etymological database, record 299, ‘fall’.**
    [Original record](https://starlingdb.org/cgi-bin/response.cgi?basename=%2Fdata%2Fdrav%2Fdravet&root=config&single=1&text_number=299).
    Confirms that PDr *ḍol-* is an upstream reconstruction. Its status is
    a substantive comparative question, distinct from the lost uncertainty
    before Telugu *ūḍu* in DEDR 2988.

24. **Thomas, Jens. DHARMA edition INSTelugu00101, land donation by

25. **Thomas, Jens. DHARMA edition INSTelugu00040.**
    [Edition](https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSTelugu00040).
    Last content modification checked: 26 August 2026, `f40e962`.

    the earlier edition's *ḷe-*. This is a useful warning about evidential
    dependence, not independent proof of the predicted vowel length.

## Reproducibility and limits

**Indirect reference:** Burrow, T. 1976. “A Sketch of Manda Grammar in
Comparison with Pengo.” *Dravidian Linguistics V*, 39–56. DEDR 2456 cites
pp. 40–41 for the comparison involving Kui *jāpa* and Manda *jē-/hē-*.
The original article was **not obtained**. The essay reports the DEDR
placement and attribution, not an independent reading of Burrow's argument.

The local inventory records checksums for `forms.csv`, `edges.csv`,
`languages.csv`, `pdr-headword-audit.csv`, and the legacy `pdr.csv` in
`inventory-summary.json`. It retains stable form IDs and immediate parents.
The 116 initial-cluster groups were manually triaged; they are not a gold
standard of 116 metatheses. Root-initial apical cases and unchanged controls
were examined separately in the casebook. No success rate for all eligible
Telugu derivatives is claimed.

An access page saved with a `.pdf` filename is not a consulted article.
In particular, complete texts were not obtained for Krishnamurti 1955 and
1978, Hume's final 2004 article, or Emeneau 1971. Later discussions and
the accessible preprint are identified where they supply the argument.
