# Reading log and interpretive safeguards

These are working notes, not a transcript of the sources. Page numbers are printed
page numbers unless explicitly marked PDF. Reconstructions in these notes remain
attributed proposals, not new database ancestry assertions.

## Krishnamurti 1961, *Telugu Verbal Bases*

Read in the publisher-authorized Google Books preview of the later reprint,
book ID `56PXtopfoscC`. The page numbering agrees with citations in the 2003 book.

- pp. 51–52, §§1.121–124: the original account distinguishes an immediately
  consonantal derivative from a vowel-initial derivative. The former gives
  movement with doubling of a following singleton; the latter gives movement
  and vowel contraction. This is more informative than applying a two-vowel
  formula indiscriminately to every dictionary headword.
- p. 52: `iẓ-g > ẓi-gg > ḍiggu/ḍigu`, and `iẓ-nd > ẓi-nd > ḍindu`.
  Gemination is not required when a cluster is already present. The form of a
  suffix and the identity of the root are separate reconstruction questions.
- p. 53, §1.126: initial `ḍ` in `ḍakku` is explicitly uncertain. A proposed
  retroflex nasal has no direct cognate evidence; an alternative is literary
  hyperstandardization of `dakku` after the `ḍ > d` change. This is an excellent
  warning against inventing a proto-segment solely to save a sound law.
- pp. 53–54: `ḍaṅgu` ‘be pounded’ admits more than one analysis. The proposal
  involving shortening of a contracted long vowel is supported by a long-vowel
  Kui form, but it is not a uniquely recovered history.
- p. 54: long vowel + nasal–stop may give either nasal loss or vowel shortening.
  `loṅgu/lōgu` illustrates the alternatives. The claimed avoidance of homonymy
  is a hypothesis, not direct evidence of a speaker's motivation.
- p. 54, §1.127: alternative derivations for `ḍullu/ḍollu` differ in suffix
  vowel and the choice between syncope and contraction followed by shortening.
  The source itself leaves this underdetermined.
- p. 55, §1.129: older literary `ḍ-` increasingly competes with `d-` across the
  11th–14th centuries; literary survivals prevent a sharp date for completion.
  The proposed early loans into neighboring languages preserve `ḍ`.
- p. 55, §1.130: short `ruddu` and `rubbu` are related to a root `*ur-` with
  different consonantal derivatives. Do not derive one from the other by an
  unmotivated stop substitution.
- pp. 61–62, §§1.143–144 (visually reread): high derivative `u` is lost,
  especially before NP/PP, while `a` remains. The contraction table explicitly
  includes `i…i/u > ī` and `u…u/i > ū`, unlike the 2003 shorthand. The author
  explicitly leaves the existence of an uncontracted hiatus stage in Telugu
  unknown, citing Kui hiatus as a parallel in footnote 78 (checked below).
  Page 61 also explicitly separates **non-morphemic final u** from derivative
  vowels. A word-final citation vowel is not automatically the contracting V2.
- pp. 65–66, §1.153: the class of voiced-geminate formations may *probably*
  reflect early Kannada borrowing, followed by metathesis inside Telugu.
  `perggaḍa/preggaḍa` in 9th–10th century inscriptions is supporting chronology,
  not proof that every member is borrowed.
- p. 66, §1.154 (visually reread): two alternative explanations of the doubled
  following consonant: old post-r consonant length (orthographic doubling is
  attested, but its phonetic interpretation uncertain); or pressure toward
  CV̄C / CVCC stem shapes rather than CVC. Illustrates `*kaẓ-m-` becoming a
  geminate formation, and compares independent base-final consonant doubling
  in Dravidian. Do not present only one of these as an observed chronology.
- p. 67, §1.156: `vrīlu` is assigned `*vir-il`; the author explicitly admits
  that no language attests this exact derivative `-il/-ul`. It fits a prediction
  about a high suffix vowel, but is not independent verification of that vowel.
- pp. 67–68: long outcomes include low-vowel nasal-stop derivatives; the nasal
  can subsequently be lost leaving nasalization. Distinguish metathesis from
  this later development. The early `prālūru` citation was checked against
  the original edition and DHARMA, recorded in the charter section below.
- pp. 130–131, notes 67 and 77–85, visually read: note 67 distinguishes ordinary
  Tulu aphaeresis from examples of the metathesis type, citing 'wing'. The claim
  of an identical inherited SCDr innovation nevertheless does not follow.
- Note 77 explicitly leaves relative ordering of medial-vowel weakening and
  suffix voicing undecided. A complete linear chronology would overstate the
  original argument.
- Note 78 cites Winfield's *Kui Grammar*, pp. 1–2, for separately pronounced
  adjacent vowels (including `lāa` 'young woman'). These
  illustrate hiatus generally, not necessarily intermediate reflexes of the
  very same Telugu etyma. Do not overstate them as direct attestation of an
  ancestral Telugu metathesis stage.
- **Note 80 explicitly discusses Telugu `truḷḷu` 'frisk, jump, be proud' as
  secondary r**, probably literary hyperstandardization on the pattern of
  already archaic initial clusters. Tamil, Malayalam, Kannada and Tulu `tuḷḷu`
  supply the comparison. This is not a new conjecture of this investigation,
  although the precise social mechanism remains the author's proposal.
- Note 81: Rayalaseema `minda` versus Sarkars `mīda` 'above', from Old Telugu
  `mīda < *minda`, illustrates dialect differentiation. Comparable Old Telugu
  dialect differences are inferred, not documented by this modern pair.
- Note 82 warns the displayed reconstructions are **immediate antecedents**,
  not automatically Proto-Dravidian words. This directly matters to Jambu's
  reconstruction-level audit.
- Note 83 gives `perggaḍa(luṃ)` (850 CE), *Nellore District Inscriptions* II
  no. 31, line 5; Kannada `perggaḍe` (951 CE), SII XI-1 no. 39, via Gai p. 155.
- Note 84 cautions that post-r doubled spellings probably follow a Sanskrit
  orthographic tradition. Spelling alone is not proof of phonetic gemination.
- Note 85 cites QJAHRS I-ii, p. 101, line 8, for the early inscription; the
  reprint and modern edition are examined in the charter section below.
- p. 59 §1.141, visually reread: **the 1961 account rejects accent-shift**,
  agreeing with Alfred Master against Ramaswami Aiyar, before preferring
  metathesis plus contraction. The 1978/2003 stress account is therefore a
  substantive later revision informed by Konda fieldwork, not one unchanged
  doctrine. Aiyar's older proposal assimilated the **second** vowel to the
  first, then lost the first under second-syllable stress. This progressive
  quality assimilation is separate from earlier i/u lowering before a.

## Peda-Vēgi charter, Nandivarman II year 10

R. Subba Rao 1926 [1948 reprint], “Pedavegi copper-plates of
Nandivarman II”, QJAHRS I(2), pp. 92–105. Downloaded complete volume from
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.3441897; text read pp. 92–103 and image checked
p. 102 = PDF p. 123 (`jahrs-pralura.png`).

- Actual romanized line 8 has `Prāluragrāme`, on **p. 102 of the reprint**.
  TVB note 85 cites p. 101; do not call this an error without checking the
  original printing. The downloaded volume's PDF p. 6 explicitly says reprint,
  printed 31 March 1948 (`jahrs-reprint-title.png`, visually checked).
  This is a place-name in a **Sanskrit charter**.
- The charter gives **regnal year 10**, without an absolute-era date. Subba
  Rao's pp. 94–95 estimate the ruler's period by genealogy (assigning 25-year
  generations) and place the end of his reign around 440 CE. Do not present
  that reconstruction as an explicitly dated fifth-century Telugu text.
- Modern primary re-edition by Arlo Griffiths and Vincent Tournier:
  https://dharmalekha.info/texts/INSEIAD00166 (version 2026-08-26, 99dfc27).
  Confirms `prāluragrāme` in line 8 and Sanskrit language. Its apparatus notes
  substantial deficiencies in the old romanization, including omitted post-r
  doubles. Original and 1948 printings differ.
- The initial `pr-` is strong textual evidence. The lexical etymology and
  date of the **onset** or completion of metathesis do not follow from this
  isolated name alone.

## Emeneau 1970, *Dravidian Comparative Phonology: A Sketch*

Downloaded from Tamil Digital Library, PDF pages 42–45 = printed pp. 33–36.
OCR damages nearly every special consonant and many vowel-length marks; verify
any form against the image or DEDR before using it.

- §15 places metathesis and contraction together but explicitly leaves their
  relative sequencing open. Its compact quantity generalization contrasts
  long vowels before a singleton with short vowels before clusters.
- Telugu permits fewer second members of initial clusters than Kui/Kuwi.
- The blanket statement of later Telugu cluster-r loss carries an explicit
  qualification: it does not cover all instances.
- The examples include morphology and dialect distinctions that a flat modern
  word list would erase. DED numbers here are from the earlier edition, not
  automatically DEDR numbers.

## Krishnamurti 2003, *The Dravidian Languages*

- pp. 99–117: vowel quantity, umlaut, contraction, and high-vowel loss.
- p. 128: metathesis precedes Telugu palatalization in the proposed relative
  chronology; `krinda` protects `k` from the front vowel. Initial voicing and
  expressive vocabulary provide other reasons for non-palatalization.
- pp. 130–131: initial voicing is favored in several environments but has
  numerous counterexamples. It cannot be promoted to an exceptionless rule
  `kr > gr`, much less reconstructed as inherited initial voicing.
- pp. 157–163, Rule 20: compare the long and short branches with the more
  morphologically explicit 1961 formulation. The short branch's displayed
  conditions should not be used to generate every attested short outcome.
- p. 189: the derivation of `digu` explicitly includes consonant gemination and
  later degemination. This independent discussion prevents dropping these
  stages merely because the compact Rule 20 omits them.
- Terminology: South Dravidian II here = South-Central Dravidian. `R` in the
  apical-displacement rule excludes nasals. The historical non-nasal class is
  broader than modern phonetic rhotics.
- pp. 157–158: the prose cross-references to Rule 20's lettered branches are
  internally problematic: it pairs `20a,b` with unrestricted R, then `20a′,b′`
  with C1 and restricted R. The actual reflex table and the older 1961 account
  distinguish vowel-initial from consonant-initial formations. Do not encode
  the prose's lettering literally into a sound-change engine.
- p. 158 versus p. 160: the first says the rules ceased in Telugu, Gondi and
  Konda; the second explicitly gives ongoing dialectal Konda cluster formation.
  The latter is supported by the actual Araku/Sova contrast, and the general
  cessation claim requires qualification.
- pp. 162–163: restricted extensions include dental `d` and labial `w` in
  demonstrative/oblique paradigms, Classical Telugu `an-` 'say' with nasal-initial
  alternants, Konda/Pengo/Manda 'daughter' from `*magaḷ`, and the difficult
  'urinate' comparison with `*um(b)-uḷ`. These do not justify unrestricted R.
- pp. 116–117: Tulu and Irula aphaeresis is explicitly not established as the
  same historical innovation. Tulu includes a transparent Sanskrit loan
  `upavāsa` with initial-vowel loss. Native apical-initial forms outside SCDr
  cannot simply be called Telugu loans.

## Krishnamurti 1980, “A Vowel-Lowering Rule in Kui–Kuvi”

https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/BLS/article/view/2099
DOI 10.3765/bls.v6i0.2099. Printed pp. 495–506; rendered and OCRed locally.

- The key conditioning is the *low second vowel*, not merely the fact that a
  long mid vowel is derived by contraction. K80's `*koẓ-a` > Kui `krāu` can be
  compared with PSS83 p. 236's `*koẓ-uw` > `krōga`; the latter is an additional
  control, not one of K80's numbered examples. K80's `*ner-u` > `drē` is another
  high-vowel control.
- Inherited long mid vowels in 'river/water', 'burn', and 'stick' do not share
  the lowering. 'Skin', with earlier contraction, is also an explicit residual
  counterexample requiring relative chronology, not suppression.
- Proposed open mid allophones `ɛː/ɔː` preserve a contrast until merger with
  `ā`; otherwise a historical rule would need to know the past history of
  already merged vowels. Precise intermediate values are hypothetical.
- Exceptions such as 'bone' and the chironji tree remain. Different derivatives,
  chronology, and lexical diffusion are alternatives to examine, not automatic
  explanations available whenever the rule fails.
- **Internal arithmetic error, p. 499**: the numbered distribution lists nine
  Kui-only cases, one Kuvi-only case (explicitly no Kui cognate), six Kui+Kuvi,
  and two Kui+Kuvi+Manda. The prose instead says 18/18 Kui and ten Kui-only.
  Image `k1980-06.png` checked. Do not repeat the incorrect 100% or 56% figures.
- Footnote 4 p. 505 explicitly qualifies Telugu cluster reduction: the first
  member disappears when it is /w/ and the following vowel is low. Compare
  `vrāyu > rāyu` with `vrēlu > vēlu`.

## Steever (ed.) 2020 [ebook 2019], *The Dravidian Languages*, second edition

User-local PDF in Downloads. Extracted for research, not redistributed.

- Krishnamurti's Telugu chapter, pp. 260–261: relative historical changes;
  early apical displacement and palatalization, subsequent coronal mergers,
  nasalization, and cluster reduction. Lists `trāsu` 'balance' among medieval
  Arabic/Persian loans. Donor history should be checked independently before
  using it as a detailed metathesis example.
- Krishnamurti and Benham's Konda chapter, pp. 296–297: direct dialect evidence
  `maran : mrānu`, `peṛel : pṛēl`; also `ṛānu` 'bull' from a `saṛa/saṛanu`
  formation, distinct from the ‘tree’ comparison. The migration model inferred from their distribution
  is a hypothesis; the recorded dialect contrast is the stronger observation.
- Steever's Kuvi chapter, pp. 352, 355, confirms nasal extension and the plural
  `pēnu : pṇē-ka`, explicitly noting retroflexion. Also `ḍōri : ḍrō-ka` and
  verb alternations. The chapter draws on Israel 1979 and other descriptions;
  it is not a newly independent field sample of those same examples.
- Kuvi pp. 352–353: voiced/voiceless distinction rather than phonemic geminate
  contrast; final-vowel loss and other plural morphophonemics. Cluster metathesis
  `kp > pk` is morphologically restricted and distinct from apical displacement.
- Important: the 2003 historical statement that nasals do not participate cannot
  be read as a universal claim about all later Kuvi morphophonology.

## Kujur and Dash 2025, Kunha

https://doi.org/10.25077/ar.12.2.114-124.2025

- New field study of the Kurux-related variety Kunha/Kisan; ten older male
  speakers in Sundergarh/Sambalpur. Claimed 5,000-item collection; only the
  selected comparisons are exposed in the article.
- Many outcomes move a *final* consonant left of a vowel and favor vowel-final
  words, without the first-two-syllable contraction of the SCDr pattern.
  Example `nalakh : nalkhu` 'work'. These may involve additional vowel changes
  and morphological differences; Modern Kurux is not itself the ancestor of
  Kunha.
- Analyses are uneven: some pairs described as pure transposition change
  segment identities or contain inconsistent segment labels (e.g. `endraː :
  endeːr` described as `en > ne`). Cite the observed pairs cautiously; do not
  import the claim of an unrestricted inherited pan-Dravidian rule.

## Emeneau 1997, Brahui

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0041977X00032481
User-local PDF, matching the existing Jambu source's checksum.

- p. 445 §12: `tāring` 'sift' supports placing `dranz-/drāz-` in DEDR 3195,

  metathesis and refers to 1971 pp. 192–194 for conditioning. Full 1971 account

## DEDR, introduction

https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/burrow/frontmatter/frontmatter.html

- §2: the dictionary deliberately supplies etymological groups rather than
  proto-language reconstructions; substantial uncertainty remains in vowels.
- §§4–5: spelling can obscure the historical `r/ṟ` distinction; one group may
  contain distinct derivatives, proto-variants, or vowel-length allomorphs.
- §6: borrowing among Dravidian languages is included in groups, frequently
  without an explicit note. Group membership does not itself prove inheritance.
- §7: some members have unexplained phonology and are included provisionally
  because of meaning. A graph edge cannot make these proposals certain.
- §9: definite Indo-Aryan loans are largely excluded from the main dictionary.
  DEDR alone is therefore a biased dataset for testing loan diagnostics.

## Blevins and Garrett 2004; Garrett and Blevins 2009

- The former treats historical Dravidian apical displacement as a possible
  perceptual metathesis; extended acoustic cues can make their timing ambiguous.
- The latter's detailed analogical reanalysis concerns Kondh `kp > pk` and
  `gb > bg` in morphology. It is not a refutation of vowel–apical displacement.
  Keep the two phenomena separate even when both occur in a language.

## Subrahmanyam 1983, *Dravidian Comparative Phonology*

Full public scan found at
https://archive.org/details/SubrahmanyamDravidianComparativePhonology1983 .
501 PDF pages; main printed page = PDF page minus 30 in the sections consulted.
Archive OCR loses most underlining and some dots; images are authoritative.
Read chapters 15.2 and 16 in full, plus §6.3 on Kota. Relevant pages rendered.

- **pp. 221–223, §15.2: substantive competing chronology for Kui–Kuvi.**
  Subrahmanyam proposes `e/o > a / _C-a` **before** metathesis or glide loss,
  followed by ordinary `a+a > ā`. This dispenses with Krishnamurti's
  unattested open-mid intermediate vowel class. His supporting parallel is
  independent northern-Gondi lowering; examples include salt `*cowar >
  *cawar > sār` (Kui) versus Konda `sōr`, and hare `*moẓal > *maẓal > mrāl`
  versus Konda `morol`/Pengo `mrōl` (exact lateral symbols vary by language).
  These examples still require the proper local consonant histories.
- The two analyses make different predictions about *uncontracted* eligible
  forms and the relative date of each contraction. The a-conditioned outcome
  itself is better established than either absolute chronology. Do not treat
  an unattested quality split as fact or reject it merely for being unattested;
  it may preserve an otherwise required contrast. Conversely, the northern
  Gondi parallel demonstrates phonetic plausibility, not common inheritance
  of the Kui change. Need test residuals under both models.
- **p. 229**: specifies a short-vowel, singleton-apical root followed by a
  formative; free roots and roots already containing long vowels, geminates,
  or clusters are excluded from the core. Explicit nonapplications include
  Telugu `eṟugu`, `perugu`, `aḍagu`. Long-root Telugu clusters therefore need
  independently supported short allomorphs or another analysis.
- pp. 230–232: phonological account differs in presentation from both TVB and
  the 2003 formulation: metathesis then V2 loss, compensatory lengthening with
  V2=a when no following cluster. Footnote 5 quotes the later 1978 stress
  account and recognizes a few cases where V1 instead disappears. This is
  evidence of real analytic alternatives, not three independent datasets.
- pp. 230–231 n. 4: inscriptional historical ẓ and retroflex lateral initially
  supply evidence of positional mergers; clusterless spellings are already
  occasionally attested in 7th–11th-century records. The source's statement
  that writing `rāyu` is the *only* first-member loss should be kept distinct
  from Krishnamurti 1980's general low-vowel conditioning.
- p. 232 n. 7: **Kuvi nasal extension** includes `mniːya` tree, `pēnu : pnēka`
  louse, `mnih-/mins-` lightning, `mīnu : mnīka` fish. These can involve
  original long root vowels and nasal C2, violating the early core's limits.
  Keep dialect labels and later morphophonology explicit.
- pp. 233–239: useful comparative vowel-initial cases: hide, fall, descend,
  young, king, mortar, plough, tiger, deer, inside, seventy. The list marks
  various Kolami/Naiki/Gadaba/Kannada forms as Telugu loans rather than
  independent applications. Those diagnoses are claims to weigh, not
  automatic labels generated from the spelling.
- **p. 240, example 805**: Telugu `gōru` nail is explicitly included as a
  non-apical exception, with hypothetical `*okar`; Tamil `ukir`, Kannada
  `ugur`, Tulu `uguru` support the larger comparison. The `o` and assumed
  low formative are not automatically reconstructed from those high-vowel
  forms. Nails cannot be omitted merely because it weakens an apicals-only
  generalization.
- pp. 240–241: further extensions include `kā-` alongside `agu-/avu-` become,
  `nā-n` say, daughter, urinate, and several personal/deictic pronoun bases.
  Their morphological distribution and sometimes disputable segmentation
  justify keeping them outside an unrestricted lexical sound law.
- **pp. 241–244**: first-member loss is broader in some languages than the
  compact 2003 Rule 21b's n/w/s formulation, including g/t/p/m. The common
  core is especially strong for initial n; exact language and word must be
  specified for the extensions. Do not suppress `forget` and `daughter` just
  because their C1 is m. Retention in some related forms is real evidence of
  incomplete distribution.
- **pp. 244–245, §16.2**: seven Brahui metathesis/aphaeresis sets attributed
  to Emeneau 1971 pp. 192–194. C1=t in five, p in one, absent in one; no k/c
  examples in the sample. The formula requires following C3(C4); `pirG-`
  twist remains an explicit nonapplication. Two examples favor metathesis
  over bare aphaeresis; the other five admit either. Printed forms include
  `trukk-` pluck vs Kurux `turux-`, `rūs-` pull vs Tamil `iẓu`, `trump/tramp`
  drop vs Tamil `cōri/cori`, `trikk-` sprout vs Tamil `taẓai`, a homophonous
  `trikk-` wither vs Tamil `tiraṅku`, `trujj-` choke vs Tamil `turu`, and
  `princ-` squeeze vs Tamil `piẓi`. These are old DED numbers, not DEDR.
  Read image `pss-275.png`. The 1997 `dranz-/drāz-` study adds later evidence;
  seven is the 1971 sample, not a timeless total for Brahui.
- pp. 246–248: Tulu and Irula aphaeresis includes nasal C2; source distinctions
  between Tulu social dialects matter. Kannada/Kodagu 'wing' and 'eyelid'
  illustrate superficially similar initial liquids without demonstrating the
  same SCDr event. Tamil/Malayalam also have cluster-producing late aphaeresis
  (`pilā/plā`, `nilā/nlā/lā`), so even a new initial cluster is insufficient.
- **pp. 81–87, §6.3: Kota glide metathesis.** Earlier noninitial i > y,
  followed in selected phonological/morphological environments by Cy > yC.
  Secondary-stem and feminine suffix y behave differently from nonmorphemic
  y; uː and dorsal contexts favor some alternations, with explicit exceptions.
  p. 86 distinguishes proposed later Badaga loans with unshifted y from older
  Indo-Aryan loans which did undergo the change (including `cōym` god and
  `caynt` market). A useful independent analogue for loan chronology, not
  the same apical-displacement rule. Source's strong `must be loans` inference
  should be weakened where metathesis alone is the only evidence.

## Final source-dependency audit

The separate `vowel-lowering-audit.md` supersedes preliminary count-only
comments above. It checks K80's 18 positives and seven numbered controls,
plus the additional fat comparison from PSS83 p. 236.

- **K80 item 5:** the printed Kuvi `rec-/re'-/rēph-` forms do not display
  the `ā` that the summary table counts. More substantively, DEDR 2456
  places Kui `jāpa` and Manda `jē-/hē-` with Tamil `cāy`, citing Burrow 1976
  pp. 40–41. DEDR 516 retains the other descent comparison. The original
  Burrow article was not obtained; the competing DEDR placement is directly
  checked and is not equivalent to independently endorsing Burrow's argument.
- **K80 item 2 and n. 3:** the ‘young’ assignment was selected partly by
  the lowering theory, and DEDR explicitly cites K80. Those two publications
  are not independent confirmations of the low-vowel input. Manda is assigned
  to this item in the distribution summary without a token in its main entry.
- **K80 item 9:** printed old DED 1628 is an erroneous reference; old 1678
  corresponds to current DEDR 2018 ‘family/kindred’. Old 1628 is ‘do’.
- **K80 item 16:** the `mārcca` versus PSS83/DEDR `marcu` quantity discrepancy
  must be retained. Item 18 repeats the language label Kui; DEDR independently
  supplies Kuvi forms, but does not make the original printed label correct.
- **Bone:** Konda `peṛen` has a second `e`. The low vowel in `*peẓan` is a
  hypothesis, not an independently attested input disproved by `pṛēnu`.
- **Positive correction:** DEDR 4536 credits K80 for separating ‘sell’ from
  ‘spread’. The same paper can supply a useful separation and weak individual
  comparisons; the audit evaluates each rather than endorsing or rejecting
  the whole paper.
- **Winfield p. 10, image checked:** `kiru : krīka` ‘ear(s)’ and
  `priu : prīnga` ‘roll(s) of string’. Do not replace the recorded `kiru`
  with an invented `kriu` intermediate. The paradigm supplies phonetic
  parallels, not independently attested ancestral Telugu stages.
- **K61 p. 56:** the older `rālu` comparison invokes Tamil `aṟ-al` ‘become
  detached by cutting’. That Tamil comparison is absent from present DEDR
  233; the casebook therefore makes the fuller `-al` input conditional on
  accepting the older grouping.
- **Positional Central Dravidian comparisons:** in DEDR 502 the unchanged
  forms are Parji `iṛv-`, Ollari `iṛg-`, and Gadaba `iḍg-`, not three Parji
  variants. In 688, Parji `uṛ-` and explicitly Telugu-borrowed `ḍukki` make
  a particularly useful local inheritance/loan contrast.
- **DHARMA INSTelugu00040:** the editor's macron in `ḷēnṟurājula` is partly
  justified by the metathesis theory. The onset and inferred quantity have
  different evidential weights. The spelling cannot independently prove
  the lengthening invoked to select its reading.
