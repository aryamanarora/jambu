A word in Jambu is a meeting point for several kinds of evidence: a spelling, a meaning, a language, a proposed history, and the source that recorded it. Reading those pieces together is more useful than treating a search result as an isolated translation.

## Start with a recorded form

Consider [](form:f_2rrscalqvlihm), a form meaning ‘water’ in [](language:H). Its record gives you a specific place to start. The word itself links to the entry page; the language link opens the wider collection. These are different views of the same evidence.

The records collected below this post preserve the database’s language labels, transcription, and source locators where available. They make it possible to return from the prose to the evidence behind it.

## Follow the proposed history

This form is attached to [](entry:8082), ‘water’. The entry page lets you follow the ancestry and inspect other forms grouped under that headword. The source [](ref:CDIAL) supplies a bibliographic starting point for this dictionary material.

An etymological grouping is an analysis, not just a list of similar spellings. Pay attention to the relation on each record: a reflex, a borrowing, and a variant do not make the same historical claim. Source notes and alternate proposals matter when evaluating a connection.

## Change the question: from history to meaning

The concept [](concept:948) offers another way into the collection. It brings together forms by meaning, allowing you to compare across etymological groups and languages. Sharing a concept does not establish common ancestry.

Try moving between these views: start with a form, follow its etymology, then open the concept to see the broader semantic comparison. Return to the cited source when a transcription or historical proposal needs closer inspection.
